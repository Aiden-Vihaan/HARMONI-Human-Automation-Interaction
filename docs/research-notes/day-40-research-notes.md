HARMONI — Day 40

Robustness and Quality-Control Research Notes

---

Research Objective

Day 40 evaluates whether the descriptive analytical result is transparent and reasonably robust to participant-level variation.

---

Main Principle

A mean difference alone is insufficient to describe a repeated-measures dataset.

The participant-level distribution must also be inspected.

---

Missingness

Missing paired observations are explicitly reported.

They are not treated as zero.

They are not silently removed from the research narrative.

---

Mean and Median

Mean and median provide complementary descriptive information.

A substantial difference between them may indicate that the distribution is asymmetric or contains extreme observations.

It does not, by itself, prove a particular distributional shape.

---

Potential Extreme Observations

The IQR rule is used as a diagnostic mechanism.

An observation outside the IQR-based boundaries is flagged for review.

It is not automatically removed.

---

Sensitivity Analysis

A secondary summary can be compared with the primary mean.

The purpose is to understand whether the descriptive pattern is sensitive to the summary method.

---

Directional Consistency

The proportion of participant differences sharing the same direction as the aggregate result provides an additional participant-level perspective.

It is descriptive.

It is not a significance test.

---

Research Integrity

HARMONI does not optimize analytical choices to produce a preferred result.

Primary analysis remains predefined.

Sensitivity analysis remains secondary.

---

Conclusion

Day 40 adds a quality-control layer that makes the analytical system more transparent.

The result is no longer represented only as:

"Condition B − Condition A = X"

Instead, HARMONI can also communicate:

- how many participants contributed,
- how much data was missing,
- how variable the participant differences were,
- whether potential extreme observations exist,
- and whether the descriptive summary changes under a secondary diagnostic.
