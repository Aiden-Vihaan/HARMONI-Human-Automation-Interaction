# HARMONI — Day 36
## Participant Difference Distribution Research Notes

---

# Research Question

Does a condition-level average adequately describe behavioural differences between experimental conditions?

---

# Observation

It may not.

A condition-level mean can conceal substantial participant-level heterogeneity.

Example:

P001: +800 ms
P002: +700 ms
P003: -600 ms
P004: +100 ms

A single mean cannot communicate the complete pattern.

---

# Participant-Level Differences

For each participant:

difference = Condition B − Condition A

This produces one participant-level observation.

---

# Why This Is Useful

The participant-level difference preserves:

- direction
- magnitude
- participant variability
- within-subject structure

---

# Signed Differences

Positive:

Condition B > Condition A

Negative:

Condition B < Condition A

Zero:

Condition B = Condition A

---

# Absolute Differences

Absolute difference:

|Condition B − Condition A|

describes magnitude only.

It must not replace the signed difference because it removes direction.

---

# Distribution

The collection of participant-level differences can be summarized using:

mean

median

minimum

maximum

standard deviation

---

# Directional Analysis

The number of:

positive

negative

zero

differences

is also retained.

This allows a descriptive assessment of directional consistency.

---

# Missing Pairs

A participant missing either condition cannot contribute a valid within-participant difference.

Such participants are counted separately as missing.

---

# Statistical Unit

The primary unit for Day 36 is:

participant

not:

trial.

---

# Sample Size

If 20 participants produce valid paired observations:

n = 20

even if those participants collectively completed hundreds of trials.

---

# Heterogeneity

Consider:

P001: +500 ms
P002: +500 ms
P003: +500 ms

This indicates strong directional consistency.

Compare with:

P001: +1000 ms
P002: -1000 ms
P003: 0 ms

The same absolute magnitude can occur with very different directional patterns.

Therefore signed differences are retained.

---

# Mean vs Median

Mean difference describes the arithmetic average.

Median difference describes the central ordered observation.

If the distribution is skewed, they may differ substantially.

Both are therefore retained.

---

# Outliers

No automatic outlier removal is performed.

Extreme participant differences remain visible.

---

# Missingness

Missing paired observations are explicitly counted.

They are not replaced with zero.

---

# Interpretation Boundary

The system reports observed differences.

It does not claim:

- statistical significance
- causality
- psychological mechanism

without an appropriate study design and statistical procedure.

---

# Research Transparency

A complete difference summary should expose:

valid participant count

missing participant count

mean difference

median difference

standard deviation

positive count

negative count

zero count

---

# Conclusion

Day 36 moves HARMONI from simple condition averages toward participant-aware effect-oriented descriptive analysis.

The project can now describe not only:

"How different were the conditions on average?"

but also:

"How consistently did participants differ between the conditions?"

This provides a stronger foundation for later inferential analysis.
