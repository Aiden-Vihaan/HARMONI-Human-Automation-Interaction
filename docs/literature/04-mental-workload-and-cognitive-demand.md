# Literature Review 04 — Mental Workload and Cognitive Demand

## HARMONI Research Area

Mental Workload in Human–Automation Interaction

---

# 1. Purpose

Mental workload is an important Human Factors construct for understanding how demanding an interaction is for a person.

HARMONI investigates how information presented by an automated system may influence human understanding, decision-making, and intervention.

However, providing additional information may also increase the amount of information that a person needs to process.

Therefore, the project must consider both:

- Information usefulness
- Cognitive demand

The central principle for this stage of the literature review is:

> More information does not necessarily mean better human–automation interaction.

---

# 2. Working Definition of Mental Workload

Mental workload can be understood as the level of cognitive resources required to perform a task under particular conditions.

It is influenced by multiple factors, including:

- Task complexity
- Information quantity
- Time pressure
- Environmental demands
- Interface design
- Prior knowledge
- Experience
- Concurrent tasks

A simplified representation is:

Task Demands
     +
Interface Demands
     +
Environmental Demands
     ↓
Mental Workload
