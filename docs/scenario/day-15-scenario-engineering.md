## Scenario Engineering Specification
**Status:** Scenario Architecture Defined

---

# 1. Purpose

Day 15 converts the experimental methodology into a concrete, reproducible interactive scenario.

The objective is to create a controlled Human-Automation Interaction environment in which participants must:

1. Monitor an automated system.
2. Observe a developing environmental situation.
3. Receive an automation limitation message.
4. Interpret the available information.
5. Decide whether intervention is required.
6. Perform the appropriate intervention.
7. Answer Situation Awareness questions.
8. Report perceived workload.

The scenario must create a genuine requirement for situation understanding rather than simple button memorisation.

---

# 2. Scenario Concept

HARMONI will use a simplified automated navigation environment.

The participant supervises an automated vehicle travelling through a controlled virtual environment.

The vehicle normally operates autonomously.

During selected trials, the system encounters a situation that exceeds its current automation capability.

The participant must understand the situation and appropriately assume control.

---

# 3. Why a Simplified Environment?

The objective is not to build a realistic driving simulator.

The objective is to study:

> Human understanding of automation limitations under different communication conditions.

Therefore, unnecessary real-world complexity will be excluded.

The environment will contain only variables necessary to create:

- Automation state
- Environmental context
- Developing risk
- Automation limitation
- Human intervention
- Measurable outcomes

---

# 4. Experimental Scenario

The baseline scenario is:

> An automated vehicle is travelling along a controlled roadway while monitoring its environment.

The participant acts as the human supervisor.

The vehicle maintains autonomous operation until an environmental event develops.

A relevant obstacle enters the predicted path.

The automation detects that its current control capability is insufficient.

The system communicates the limitation to the participant.

The participant must determine the appropriate response.
