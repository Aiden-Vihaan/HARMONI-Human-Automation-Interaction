# HARMONI — Problem Statement

## Background

Advanced driver-assistance and automated-driving systems increasingly perform parts of the driving task on behalf of humans.

While these systems can potentially reduce workload and provide driving assistance, they also introduce an important Human Factors challenge: the human driver must maintain an appropriate understanding of the system's capabilities, limitations, and current state.

The effectiveness of automation therefore depends not only on technical capability, but also on how effectively the system communicates with its human operator.

---

## Problem

Drivers may develop an inaccurate understanding of what an automated-driving system is currently doing or what it is capable of handling.

Conventional warning interfaces may communicate that an event has occurred without providing sufficient contextual information about:

- What the system detected
- What the system is currently doing
- Why the system is requesting intervention
- What the system cannot handle
- What action the human should take

This may contribute to inappropriate reliance, delayed intervention, excessive workload, or reduced confidence in automation.

---

## Research Problem

HARMONI investigates whether contextual and explainable automation feedback can support better human understanding of automated-driving system states and improve driver responses during situations requiring human intervention.

The project focuses on the interaction between automation transparency and human behaviour rather than on autonomous-driving algorithms themselves.

---

## Proposed Approach

HARMONI will develop a browser-based experimental driving simulation containing controlled automated-driving scenarios.

Two interface conditions will be compared:

1. A conventional/minimal automation feedback interface.
2. An explainable/contextual automation feedback interface.

Participant behaviour and subjective responses will then be evaluated using measures related to:

- Reaction time
- Decision accuracy
- Situation awareness
- Trust
- Workload
- Confidence
- Perceived safety

---

## Intended Contribution

The project aims to demonstrate how Human Factors principles can be incorporated into the design and evaluation of intelligent human–machine interfaces.

The final outcome will consist of:

- A research-oriented prototype
- An experimental methodology
- A participant dataset
- Statistical analysis
- Human Factors findings
- Evidence-based design recommendations

---

## Research Position

HARMONI treats the interface not merely as a visual layer but as a communication mechanism between an intelligent automated system and a human operator.

The central design question is therefore:

> **How can an automated system communicate its state and limitations in ways that help humans understand, decide, and intervene appropriately?**
